What this calculator does

It measures the actual overlap between your selected date range and weekly work schedule. It skips disabled weekdays and loaded or manual public holidays, and uses the selected IANA time zone for daylight-saving-aware elapsed time.

Useful for

  • SLA response and resolution windows
  • Support-ticket and service deadlines
  • Project, contractor and billable hours
  • Business hours between timestamps

Example

Mon 09:00 → Fri 17:00 = 40 business hours
Fri 15:00 + 10 business hours = Mon 17:00
Mon 22:00 → Tue 06:00 = 8 overnight business hours

Business Hours Between Two Dates

To calculate business hours between two date-and-time values, the calculator checks each local calendar day in the selected time zone. It counts only the minutes that overlap an enabled work period, then excludes public holidays and custom closure dates. Partial first and last days are clipped to the exact times you enter.

How working hours are calculated

Each work period is measured as a real time interval in the selected IANA time zone. This matters for overnight shifts and daylight-saving changes: a shift crossing a clock change can contain a different number of elapsed minutes than its wall-clock labels suggest. Repeated or non-existent local clock times are rejected so the result is not based on a silent guess.

Tuesday 13:00 → Wednesday 12:00, with 09:00–17:00 hours = 7 business hours
Friday 16:00 → Monday 10:00, with 09:00–17:00 hours = 2 business hours

SLA Business Hours Calculator and Deadline Examples

Service-level agreements often measure response or resolution targets in business hours rather than continuous calendar time. Enter when a ticket or task starts, select the SLA target, and the calculator advances only through enabled work periods. Nights, non-working weekdays and excluded holidays pause the clock.

Example: an 8-business-hour response target

If a ticket arrives on Friday at 16:00 and your calendar runs Monday–Friday from 09:00 to 17:00, one business hour is used on Friday. The remaining seven hours continue from Monday at 09:00, so the deadline is Monday at 16:00. If your schedule or holidays differ, the result changes to match your calendar.

The same calendar supports adding or subtracting business hours for contractor deadlines, project turnaround times and other work-time calculations.

Workday Calculator: Business Hours vs. Full Workdays

Use this workday calculator to measure scheduled working time across a date range. The result shows total business hours and the number of business days touched. A day is counted as touched when at least one configured work period overlaps your selected range; even a partial day counts as one touched day.

Convert business hours into equivalent workdays

Equivalent workdays = total business hours ÷ your standard hours in one full workday

With the default Monday–Friday schedule of 09:00–17:00 and no lunch break configured, one full workday contains 8 scheduled hours. In that schedule, 40 business hours equals 5 equivalent workdays. If you use split shifts such as 09:00–13:00 and 14:00–17:00, the scheduled day is 7 hours, so use 7 as the divisor instead.

Example: a range that starts and ends partway through a workday

With a Monday–Friday 09:00–17:00 schedule, a Tuesday 13:00 start and Wednesday 12:00 end includes 4 hours on Tuesday (13:00–17:00) and 3 hours on Wednesday (09:00–12:00). The result is 7 business hours across 2 business days touched — not 2 full workdays.

  • Set enabled days and work periods in the Work Schedule table.
  • Use two periods to exclude an unpaid lunch break.
  • Add holiday dates when a scheduled day should not count as working time.

Method & calculation notes

The calculator converts each schedule boundary and input date-time into an instant using the selected IANA time zone. It counts actual elapsed minutes that fall inside enabled work periods. Overnight intervals are split at local midnight, and the holiday calendar excludes the whole local date selected as a holiday.

Automatic public-holiday dates are supplied by Nager.Date (REST API v4). Country and first-level subdivision coverage varies; municipal holidays, employer closures and late official changes are not guaranteed. Verify important deadlines against the official local calendar, and enter any additional closures manually.

If a local start or end time is missing during a daylight-saving transition or repeated during a fall-back hour, the calculator rejects that value rather than assuming which instant was intended.

For general clock-time arithmetic without work schedules, use the Time Calculator.
