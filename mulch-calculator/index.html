Mulch Calculator

Plan mulch for several garden beds in one project. Measure different shapes, subtract the usable mulch already there, assign products by bed, and compare supplier package or bulk offers.

Product-label first: Mulch quantity is calculated by volume. Enter the package volume or the coverage and depth printed on the actual bag. The calculator does not guess product weight, density, bag fill or a universal application depth.

1. Set your project units

Choose the units you want to enter. Individual areas and product labels can use their own supported units where shown.

2. Add garden beds and areas

Give each area a name, shape, target total depth and measured usable existing depth. Use a ring for a tree bed so the trunk area is excluded. For irregular borders, split them into non-overlapping simple shapes or use a measured custom area.

3. Define mulch products and supplier offers

A different product can be assigned to each bed. Use the package label for volume or stated coverage. Supplier prices, currency, delivery, tax and order rules are entered by you; no live prices are assumed.

4. Calculate your project

The calculator keeps each bed separate, then groups quantities only for beds assigned to the same named product.

How the mulch calculator works

For each bed, the calculator measures its net surface area and works out the extra depth needed to reach your target. It then calculates volume for that bed and adds any allowance you explicitly enter. Beds assigned to the same product are grouped; unlike products are kept separate.

Added volume = net area × max(0, target depth − usable existing depth)

For a rectangular bed, area is length × width. A circular bed uses π × radius². A tree ring uses the outer circle minus the inner circle. A triangle uses base × height ÷ 2. Custom-area mode uses an area you have already measured.

Bag quantity = round up(required product volume ÷ labelled package volume)

When a bag gives coverage instead of volume, package volume is derived from the stated coverage area multiplied by its stated coverage depth. A supplier bulk offer is rounded according to the minimum and increment you enter. The full quote comparison includes material price, delivery and the entered tax percentage.

Worked example: topping up more than one bed

Suppose a rectangular border is 10 ft by 4 ft, its measured usable existing mulch depth is 1 in, and the target total depth you choose is 3 in. The added depth is 2 in, so the geometric requirement is approximately 6.67 cubic feet (0.247 cubic yards) before any optional allowance. If a second tree ring uses a different product, it is calculated separately and never merged into the first product's bag count.

This is a demonstration of the arithmetic, not a recommendation that every garden bed should receive a 3-inch layer. Choose depth based on your plant, existing mulch, local conditions and reliable horticultural guidance.

Measure beds accurately before ordering

Measure each bed separately and avoid counting overlapping sections twice. For an irregular shape, divide it into rectangles, circles and triangles that do not overlap, or enter a carefully measured net area. For a tree ring, measure the outer diameter and the inner diameter of the exclusion area. A ring calculation subtracts the middle circle from the outer circle.

Use the existing depth only where the current material is usable and appropriate to retain. The calculator subtracts the entered existing depth mathematically; it cannot inspect mulch condition, compaction, decomposition, soil level or plant needs.

Compare bag and bulk mulch purchases responsibly

Bag sizes and coverage statements differ between products. Check whether the label gives volume, such as cubic feet or litres, or coverage at a stated depth. Do not compare two products solely by their volume if their material, particle size, moisture, quality or intended use differs.

For bulk offers, enter the supplier's price unit, minimum order, increment, delivery charge and tax. Currency comparisons are only ranked when all offers for that product use the same currency. A quote missing a price is not treated as free or cheaper.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate how much mulch I need?

Measure each bed's net area, decide the target depth for that bed, subtract only the usable existing depth, then multiply the area by the additional depth. Convert the resulting volume into bags or bulk units using the actual product or supplier specification.

Does the calculator account for mulch already in the bed?

Yes. Enter the measured existing depth for each bed. The calculator subtracts that value from the bed's target total depth. A bed already at or above the entered target produces zero additional volume; this is a calculation result, not advice about whether the existing material should remain.

Can I calculate a tree ring?

Yes. Choose Ring and enter the outer diameter and inner diameter. The inner circle is excluded from the ring area. Measure the trunk or exclusion boundary rather than assuming a standard size.

Can different beds use different mulch products?

Yes. Add each product and assign it to the relevant beds. Quantities are grouped only for beds that use the same named product, so unlike products are not mixed into a single purchase total.

How are bag counts calculated?

The calculator divides required product volume by the volume derived from the actual package label, then rounds up to a whole package. If the label gives area coverage, its stated coverage depth is required to derive volume.

Does mulch have a universal weight per cubic yard?

No universal weight is assumed. Weight can vary with material, particle size, moisture and supplier. This calculator focuses on volume and only reports weight if a future, separately sourced product-specific input is implemented; the present version does not estimate weight.

Should I always apply a fixed mulch depth?

No. Depth depends on the material, plants, site and local guidance. Enter the depth appropriate to your project and consult local horticultural extension or product guidance. Avoid piling mulch against tree trunks and plant stems. Extension advice illustrates why one depth should not be hard-coded: Colorado State University Extension describes 3–4 inches for many mulches, while its tree guidance advises keeping mulch at least 6 inches from the trunk. Mississippi State University's landscape table gives 2–3 inches for examples such as pine or hardwood bark and compost, and its tree guidance discusses pulling mulch 2–3 inches back from trunks. These are context-specific examples, not universal settings; use guidance suitable for your plants and site.

Are supplier prices and product sizes updated automatically?

No. Enter the current package label and supplier quote. The calculator runs locally in your browser and does not fetch or verify live availability, prices, taxes or delivery terms.
