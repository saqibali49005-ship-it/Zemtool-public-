True hydration vs. added-water hydration

True hydration includes water contributed by the starter or levain. Added-water hydration looks only at the water poured into the mix, so it can understate the dough's total hydration when the starter is included.

How starter hydration is counted

A 100% hydration starter contains equal weights of flour and water. A 150% hydration starter contains 1.5 times as much water as flour. The calculator separates those parts before it totals the recipe.

Baker's percentages

Flour is the 100% reference. Hydration, starter and salt percentages are compared with total flour, including flour inside the starter. Flour blend shares describe the added flour only.

Custom liquids

Milk, juice and similar ingredients are not pure water. Enter the ingredient's water fraction as a decimal percentage (for example, 87% water). Only that declared fraction counts toward true hydration.

Calculation method and assumptions

  • Starter flour = starter weight ÷ (1 + starter hydration as a decimal).
  • Starter water = starter weight − starter flour.
  • True hydration = 100 × total water ÷ total flour.
  • Salt percentage = 100 × salt weight ÷ total flour.
  • Starter percentage in this calculator = starter mass ÷ total flour, not prefermented-flour percentage. Both are shown separately where relevant.

Weights are converted to grams for calculation. Display values are rounded for readability; the calculation uses the unrounded values. Custom-liquid water fractions are user-provided assumptions. Volume-to-weight conversions and fermentation-time predictions are intentionally not included.
